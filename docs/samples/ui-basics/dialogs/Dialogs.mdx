A dialog is a portal into the page's own `<body>`, so on this page it covers
the article rather than the demo. Open the sample from **Default** in the
sidebar to see it over a screen of its own.

Tick _Delete permanently_ and watch the confirming button rename itself. A
button that repeats the verb — _Move to Trash_, _Delete permanently_ — is
readable on its own; a button that says _OK_ makes the user re-read the
paragraph above it.

## Modal or aside

`ModalDialog` is the same component both times, and `displayType` picks the
form:

```tsx
<ModalDialog visible={visible} displayType={ModalDialogType.aside} onClose={close}>
  <ModalDialog.Header>Invite people</ModalDialog.Header>
  <ModalDialog.Body>…</ModalDialog.Body>
  <ModalDialog.Footer>…</ModalDialog.Footer>
</ModalDialog>
```

Use the **modal** for one blocking decision, the **aside** for a form with room
to breathe. When the right answer depends on the screen size, pass
`displayTypeDetailed` instead and give a separate answer per breakpoint —
`{ desktop: aside, tablet: aside, mobile: modal }` is the usual shape.

## Worth copying

- **`withForm` plus `onSubmit`** turns the aside into a real `<form>`, so Enter
  submits it and the footer button can be `type="submit"`. Without it, keyboard
  users have to reach for the mouse.
- **`Header`, `Body`, `Footer` are slots, not wrappers** — they carry no markup
  of their own. Passing arbitrary children instead of those three is the
  reliable way to get a dialog that ignores your layout.
- **Close, then report.** Both dialogs here fire a toast after they close, so
  the outcome survives the dialog disappearing.
- **`scale` on footer buttons** makes them share the width evenly, which is
  what the product's dialogs do on every screen.
