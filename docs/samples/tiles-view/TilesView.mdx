# 07. Tiles and the view switch

Select two tiles, then press the list icon. The selection is still there —
because the switch changes the renderer, not the data.

<Story of={TilesViewStories.Default} />

That is the whole trick behind the portal's own view switcher, and it is worth
copying verbatim: `viewAs` and `selectedIds` are separate pieces of state, and
no component owns either of them.

## `TileContainer` sorts for you

Hand it folders and files in any order. Anything whose `item` has `isFolder`
lands under `headingFolders`, the rest under `headingFiles`:

```tsx
<TileContainer
  headingFolders={<Text isBold>Folders</Text>}
  headingFiles={<Text isBold>Files</Text>}
>
  {folders.map(…)}
  {files.map(…)}
</TileContainer>
```

Leave the headings out and you get one flat grid — which is what a rooms screen
wants, since it has nothing to separate.

## Tiles in three slots

- **`element`** is the small icon in the tile's header line, next to the
  checkbox and the ⋮ button.
- **`temporaryIcon`** is the big picture in the body, shown until a real
  `thumbnail` arrives. Pass both and the thumbnail wins once it loads, which is
  how a document preview appears without the tile jumping.
- **`children`** is a `TileContent` — the caption. `isTextOverflow` on the link
  inside it is what keeps a long filename from breaking the grid.

`badges` takes any node, so the same `Badge` you used in the list works here
too; a tile and a row are two presentations of one item, not two data models.
